Studying terrestrial water cycling through geoscience and geospatial science

We combine geoscience—Earth system science and Earth system models—with geospatial science—geographical, remote-sensing, and GIS analytics—to study terrestrial water cycling.

How we work

What we ask

Core scientific questions

Three questions organize our research across terrestrial hydrology, river systems, floods, and human–water interactions.

01

How is the terrestrial water cycle changing under the joint influence of climate and human activities?

We characterize changes in terrestrial water dynamics from a spatial perspective, with particular attention to how runoff and river flows are redistributed across river networks.

02

How does the spatial configuration of river channels and floodplains regulate hydrological pulses and floods?

We investigate how channel geometry, flow pathways, and floodplain structure shape flood propagation, attenuation, storage, and spatial expression.

03

How does the spatial distribution of human development reshape river flows and flood hazards?

We study how urbanization, levees, reservoirs, and other interventions interact with river organization to alter flow dynamics and flood risk.
