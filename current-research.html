Current research · GeoWater Lab

Understanding rivers as spatially organized systems

How does the spatial organization of river systems shape terrestrial hydrological dynamics and flood–human interactions?

conveystoreredistribute

Rivers are not merely conduits that transport water downstream.

Their channel geometry, flow pathways, floodplain connectivity, and human modifications form a spatially organized system that controls how water is generated, conveyed, stored, and redistributed across landscapes.

We move beyond spatially averaged or location-independent representations toward spatially explicit descriptions of river networks, hydrological pulses, and their changing capacity to convey and accommodate water.

Three questions

Scientific questions

01

How is the terrestrial water cycle changing under the joint influence of climate and human activities?

We characterize terrestrial water dynamics from a spatial perspective, with particular attention to how runoff and river flows are redistributed across river networks.

02

How does the spatial configuration of river channels and floodplains regulate hydrological pulses and floods?

Flood propagation, attenuation, storage, and spatial expression depend on river–floodplain capacity and configuration—not solely on the magnitude of hydrological forcing.

03

How does the spatial distribution of human development reshape river flows and flood hazards?

We investigate how urbanization, levees, reservoirs, and other interventions interact with river organization to alter flow dynamics and flood risk.

Spatial organization of global rivers

Hydrological pulses and flood dynamics

How does water move, accumulate, and propagate through spatially heterogeneous river systems?

River–human interactions

How does human development alter the spatial organization and functioning of river systems?

We study how urbanization, levees, reservoirs, and other interventions reshape flows, floods, and the coupled evolution of rivers and societies.

How we work

A spatially explicit approach to hydrology

We connect new observations to underlying processes while preserving where things happen—and why location matters.

01

Earth observation

Satellite observations and remote sensing reveal rivers and their dynamics at unprecedented scales.

02

Geospatial information

Fine-scale networks, channel geometry, flow pathways, and floodplains represent physical organization.

03

Process-based + data-driven modeling

Hydrological and hydraulic models connect observations with underlying processes.

04

Spatially informed parameterization

Location-aware, spatially adaptive representations account for heterogeneous river systems.

Enabling questions

  • How can streamflow be constrained jointly in space and time?
  • How can emerging spatial information improve river-flow estimates?
  • How can models adopt location-aware parameterizations?

Long-term vision

Toward a spatially explicit understanding of the terrestrial water cycle

Our goal is to identify general principles connecting the spatial organization of river systems with the dynamics of water and floods across scales—from runoff generation, through river-network conveyance and floodplain storage, to downstream propagation and human interaction.
