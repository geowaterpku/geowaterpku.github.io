GeoWater Lab @ PKU

Current Research

Characterizing global rivers, their pulses, and flood–human interactions through spatial information, remote sensing, hydrologic modeling, and data-driven Earth system science.

Global River Networks

Building spatially explicit representations of river networks, fine-scale flow paths, and channel geometry to understand how water moves through the terrestrial system.

Runoff & Hydrologic Pulses

Reconstructing the spatial distribution of runoff and tracing how hydrologic pulses form, propagate, attenuate, and reorganize across river networks.

Flood Dynamics

Understanding flood generation from the interaction between hydrologic forcing and the conveyance, storage, and attenuation capacity of river–floodplain systems.

River Morphology

Using remote sensing and spatial analysis to characterize channel shape, hydraulic geometry, and the physical controls governing river form and function.

Human–Water Interactions

Quantifying how levees, reservoirs, urbanization, and other human interventions reshape river connectivity, flood behavior, and terrestrial water cycling.

Spatial Earth System Science

Developing spatial constraints, information-rich observations, and optimal parameterization schemes that connect satellite observations with process-based models.
